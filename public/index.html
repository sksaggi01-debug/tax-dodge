How it works

A per-unit tax hits your market every round. One player is the Buyer, the other is the Seller. Each round you secretly choose how elastic you will be. The more elastic side pushes more of the tax onto the other side, but staying flexible costs you starting points. Highest total after 5 rounds wins.

Rules and scoring
  • Your tax burden share depends on both elasticities. Buyer share = Es ÷ (Es + |Ed|). Seller share = |Ed| ÷ (Es + |Ed|).
  • Round points = Starting points − (tax you pay + your share of deadweight loss).
  • More elastic profiles dodge more of the tax but start with fewer points (keeping alternatives open is costly).
  • The tax changes each round. Bigger taxes make dodging more valuable.

What to take away

  1. The less elastic side bears more of the tax. Burden depends on elasticities, not on who writes the check to the government.
  2. Buyer share = Es/(Es+|Ed|). When both sides are equally elastic, they split the tax 50/50.
  3. Bigger taxes reward dodging. At a $1 tax, flexibility was not worth its cost. At $4–$6 it usually was.
  4. When both sides dodge, everyone loses more trade. Two elastic sides cut quantity the most, so deadweight loss is largest.
